\begin{abstract}
This supplement contains sixteen supplementary figures and 26 individual diagnostic or schematic figures retained from the earlier layout. Measured plots read the same recorded artefacts as the main text (per-run scorecards, per-frame telemetry traces, run manifests, gate verdict files and sealed-set records) by `m5_paper_figures.py`, `m5_paper_figures_ext.py` and `m5_paper_figures_extra.py`; schematics are labelled and numerical claims are tied to recorded inputs. Section S-A collects the driving-layer evidence behind Section V-E of the main text (the single-factor ladder, per-run traces, arm distributions, lane-placement deviation, safety margins and the pairing comparison); S-B collects the composition and post-processing scans behind Section V-C (dose response over five metrics, parallel-merge and threshold scans, SWA, the β=0.8 loss arm, instance coverage and the negative-side family); S-C carries the training history; S-D carries the cost ledger and the spatial-isolation audit. The reading conventions that apply to all of them are stated in the last section, including the three quantities that are UNKNOWN rather than zero.
\end{abstract}

\section*{S-A. Driving-layer evidence}
The main text reports five pre-registered driving factors, of which four were implemented and rejected
and one was refuted by prior measurement before implementation, and localises the blocker to a static
body-sweep deadlock. The figures below are the per-arm and per-frame evidence behind those
statements.
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig6_factor_ladder.png}
\caption{Driving single-factor ladder: travelled distance (a) and stall fraction (b) per arm, with worst-case body-centre-cross frames.}
\label{fig:s1}
\end{figure}
Every arm in S1 still fails the `no_stall` hard target (0 frames) and the hard gates stay 0/4;
the ladder is what justifies reporting the implemented factors as rejected rather than as partial
progress.
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig24_time_series.png}
\caption{Per-run time series (speed top, path-to-lane deviation bottom) for a baseline acceptance run and an F-E lane-gate run.}
\label{fig:s2}
\end{figure}
S2 records long intervals of low speed and a car parked off the lane centre; the F-E arm
travels further with a larger deviation. These time series alone do not identify whether the
actuator responded correctly. The corresponding planning and arbitration evidence is reported
separately in the main text.
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig25_arm_boxplots.png}
\caption{Per-arm distributions (travelled, stall fraction) for the F-A/F-C/F-E A/Bs.}
\label{fig:s3}
\end{figure}
The arm-level distributions in S3 are the raw material of the pre-registered maximum rule:
verdicts were read as the worst run per arm, and the overlap of the ranges is why the delivered
perception arm produced no separation on path availability (0.179 $\rightarrow$ 0.200).
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig26_lane_dev.png}
\caption{Per-frame lane-placement deviation in the closed-loop round (v7 vs v8; black bar = median).}
\label{fig:s4}
\end{figure}
S4 is the closed-loop counterpart of the definitional result: under the baseline definition
the median lane deviation is 0.86 m with recorded reference-point centre crossings in 8/8/8/13
frames. No reference-point crossings were observed under the adopted definition, at a cost in
availability; this does not establish full-body lane safety.
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig27_safety_margins.png}
\caption{Safety-margin distributions (closest obstacle, minimum time-to-collision, path occupancy) over four acceptance runs.}
\label{fig:s5}
\end{figure}
S5 describes recorded obstacle and path margins. The four formal acceptance runs recorded
zero collisions, off-road frames and reversing frames, but also failed full-body centre-line
constraints (12--24 crossing frames per run). The small amount of motion and these partial metrics
cannot establish driving safety or independently confirm paved-surface containment.
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig37_pairing_compare.png}
\caption{Pairing comparison: paired-frame and candidate counts per arm (dev and limited-class pools).}
\label{fig:s6}
\end{figure}
S6 quantifies the availability cost of the adopted definition at the pairing stage itself:
3--4 paired frames are lost per 117-frame evaluation. These offline counts describe a loss of
reference availability; they do not by themselves establish the cause of the closed-loop
availability difference.

\section*{S-E. Reading conventions and stated gaps}
1. \textbf{Unit of judgement.} The acceptance results are six-seed arms (mean read against the gate, every
   seed reported); the one-shot confirmation is a single checkpoint selected by index, not by score.
2. \textbf{The sealed set is consumed.} The confirmation figures and the main-text confirmation come from that
   single consumption; the instance-level probes (identity, role, reference coverage) were \textbf{not} run on it
   and are recorded as UNKNOWN, not as passes.
3. \textbf{Two definitions of "line".} The paint scope is used for the adopted verdict, the label scope
   keeps comparability with the baseline; dilation is a trade-off between the two sets (the 0 px row
   is a `mean` read, the 1/2 px rows are `strict` reads).
4. \textbf{Frame counts are confounded by motion.} `body_cross_centre_frames` scales with how far the car
   travelled; the F-C and F-E verdicts were taken under the pre-registered maximum-over-runs rule and
   a per-distance reading was pre-registered separately for later rounds.
5. \textbf{Dose response re-evaluated.} The earlier curve's identity values do not reproduce under the frozen
   protocol (the same 6$\times$ checkpoint reads 0.763 there and 0.667 here), so it was replaced by a paired-seed
   re-evaluation (seeds 42--47): 0$\times$ 0.658, 4$\times$ 0.661, 6$\times$ 0.665 --- indistinguishable --- while the recorded 8.5$\times$
   value (0.701) is higher than 6$\times$. The 8.5$\times$ checkpoints were removed in a storage pass, so that arm cannot
   be re-evaluated.
6. \textbf{No eligible negative frames on the limited-class pool}, so that pool's false-positive rate is
   not measurable (Figure S9) and is never reported as zero.
7. \textbf{Schematics are not measurements.} Schematic figures are drawn from code and document
   definitions, not from run data.
8. \textbf{Artefact retention.} Per-epoch snapshots and superseded run directories were removed in a
   storage pass (audit file `logs/_cleanup_20261006.txt`). Selected verdict files, scorecards,
   manifests, seal records and the delivered checkpoint remain locally. These summaries do not
   recover removed training states or establish complete reconstruction of every figure;
   retained input scopes and remaining gaps are recorded in the claim manifest.
